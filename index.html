about

Nicholas D’Angelo is an American artist and photographer whose work explores the relationship between physical movement and psychological experience. Working with photography, performance, and digital manipulation, he transforms gestures and fleeting bodily actions into images that move between representation and abstraction.

Drawing on his own body as a source of visual material, D’Angelo creates photographs in which motion, fragmentation, and shifts in color and tone evoke states of uncertainty, tension, vulnerability, and transformation. His work considers how images can convey experiences that resist direct expression, allowing the figure to become both a physical presence and a reflection of interior life.

D’Angelo holds an M.F.A. in Painting and 2D Design from Cranbrook Academy of Art and a B.A. in Studio Art from Montclair State University. His work has been exhibited nationally and internationally, including at Sarah Spurgeon Gallery, T Mari Gallery, IU Kokomo Art Gallery, and Glasgow Gallery of Photography. His honors include an Honorable Mention in the 2024 Chromatic Awards and a 2025 Fine Art Photography Awards nomination.

Artist Statement

My work comes from my personal experiences and emotional struggles. I use art to give these feelings a visual form, pursuing them through movement, gesture, color, and composition. I often work with analog and digital photography, but my approach is rooted in painting and the way an image can be constructed. I orchestrate situations, then allow movement and chance to shape what emerges.

I am interested in how an image can be visually beautiful and emotionally unsettling at the same time. Strong composition and compelling color matter to me, whether an image feels carefully arranged or rough and unresolved. Blur, distortion, and movement are not simply effects; they are part of the medium through which I work. I want the images to suggest that something is happening, even when the precise nature of that experience remains unclear.

My work is personal, but I do not expect viewers to share my experiences or interpret the images as I do. I hope they recognize something of the emotion behind the work, or find an emotional response of their own. Above all, I want to make images that are visually interesting and emotionally present, allowing beauty, discomfort, and uncertainty to exist together.
